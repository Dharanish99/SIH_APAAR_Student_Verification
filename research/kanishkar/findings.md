## Decisions supported by the assembled research
- Start with a narrow post-admission reporting and correction loop; keep the existing ERP authoritative for local operations.
- Retain explicit source/evidence/rule versions, institution approval and student dispute paths.
- Distinguish local accepted submission from government acknowledgement.
- New provider documentation supports a future account-linked APAAR journey, but permission and live tests are external dependencies.
- Do not apply one quota or blacklist across institution/course/year categories.
- Never promote signatures, QR codes or anomaly scores into truth or admission authority.
- Prefer deterministic checks first. The reproduced synthetic experiment found an optional statistical signal added ten useful labelled cases and ten false alerts; no field accuracy is known.
- Compare alternatives before final selection and expose unresolved assumptions.

## Pilot/evaluation needs
Compare a small permitted pilot with the institution's existing workflow: correction success, explanation comprehension, record reconciliation, false alerts, preparation/review time, accessibility and willingness to continue. Measure baseline and proposed workflow on comparable tasks. Do not infer saved time from UI screens or synthetic timing.

Require an approved purpose/role map, minimal data, narrow course/year rules, authoritative approvals, permission for integrations and a rollback/manual process. Define explicit stop conditions for wrong matches, unauthorised disclosure, unreviewable policy or unresolved student harm. A synthetic demonstration can precede these; a real deployment cannot inherit “validated” status from it.
