## How research becomes understanding

<b>Stage 1 — Orient:</b> Understand the actors, key terms, question and why the answer changes our solution.<br /><b>Stage 2 — Investigate:</b> Open original sources, record versions and applicability, and look for contradictory evidence.<br /><b>Stage 3 — Apply:</b> Work through a normal case and failure cases. Make a workflow, rule case, integration trace, experiment or threat model.<br /><b>Stage 4 — Explain:</b> Read two important primary passages yourself, write a short explanation without AI drafting it, and answer fresh questions.<br /><b>Stage 5 — Review:</b> Present a seven-minute teach-back to a human reviewer, correct gaps and record the real review.<br />AI can keep collecting evidence while a learner is unavailable. It must leave unanswered exercises and learning checks pending.

## Suggested working sessions

1. <b>Orientation — 30–45 minutes:</b> Read the assignment, learn the glossary, choose the most consequential questions.
2. <b>Evidence — 60–90 minutes initially:</b> Gather primary evidence and record unresolved access or interpretation gaps. Extend according to need.
3. <b>Application — 45–60 minutes:</b> Complete the role-specific practical task and validate its reasoning.
4. <b>Teach-back — 7 minutes plus questions:</b> Reviewer asks one unfamiliar scenario and checks sources.
5. <b>Handoff — 20–30 minutes:</b> Update findings, sources and progress, with limitations and the next owner's dependency.<br />These are planning estimates, not evidence of completed work or deadlines.

## Sources and research hygiene

Use [the repository source guide](https://github.com/Dharanish99/SIH_APAAR_Student_Verification/blob/main/docs/source-guide.md) as a discovery map, then inspect the source supporting each claim. It is not a completed evidence register.

- Government policy: original AICTE, UGC, APAAR, MeitY or Gazette text, with amendments, academic-year scope and commencement checked.
- Integration: official provider documentation plus actual access/test evidence. A brochure or FAQ does not establish API availability.
- Engineering: first-party specifications and official documentation, versioned where relevant.
- User needs: actual interviews/observations with permission and limitations, supported by institutional process documents where possible.
- Comparative claims: documented capabilities and unknowns; no invented competitor weaknesses.<br /><b>Useful verified starting pages:</b> [APAAR official FAQ](https://apaar.education.gov.in/faqs) and [OWASP ASVS](https://asvs.owasp.org/). The APAAR FAQ explains ecosystem links; it does not establish this team's integration access. ASVS offers engineering security requirements; it does not certify legal compliance.<br /><b>Access note:</b> The NAD FAQ returned a 403 during prompt preparation. Future agents must seek an accessible official alternative or record the gap, rather than claim they inspected blocked content.

## What must never be claimed without evidence

- A report proves the author understands its contents.
- A correctly formatted ID proves identity or enrolment validity.
- A repeated enrolment proves fraud.
- A QR code alone proves credential authenticity.
- A simulated provider response is a live government integration.
- A proposed benchmark, interview or human review actually happened.
- A generic privacy checklist establishes legal compliance.<br />No real student records, identifiers, secrets or private institutional exports should be uploaded to AI tools or this public repository.
