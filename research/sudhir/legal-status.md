## Commencement, not just enactment

[G.S.R. 843(E), English page 2](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf) (SUH-102) establishes three stages:

| Stage | Provisions | Status on 2026-10-26/27 |
|---|---|---|
| Publication | Section 1(2), section 2, sections 18-26, 35, 38-43 and 44(1),(3) | Commenced |
| One year after publication | Section 6(9), section 27(1)(d) | Future |
| Eighteen months after publication | Sections 3-5; 6(1)-(8),(10); 7-17; remaining section 27; 28-34; 36-37; 44(2) | Future |

Thus the main processing, notice, consent, child-data, rights and breach duties are not yet operative under this timetable. The Gazette is dated 13 November 2025, while the electronic publication stamp and MeitY catalogue use 14 November. We preserve the instrument's relative periods instead of resolving that one-day issue by assumption. It does not affect the status on the research date; confirm the precise operational compliance date before a pilot near commencement.

## What the texts require us to plan for

The [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) (SUH-101) distinguishes consent from specified legitimate uses (sections 4, 7). Sections 7(c)-(d) require an actual statutory function/disclosure obligation; they do not grant this student project government authority. Sections 6(4)-(6) address withdrawal; section 8 covers accuracy, safeguards and retention; section 12 addresses correction/erasure. Section 2(f) defines a child as under 18, and section 9 addresses children and a person with disability who has a lawful guardian. The latter is not a rule that every person with a disability lacks decision-making capacity. Section 44(2)'s omission of IT Act section 43A is itself in the future stage, so earlier applicable protections must still be assessed.

The [final Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf) (SUH-103), rule 1 on printed page 24, commence rules 1,2,17-21 at publication, rule 4 after one year, and rules 3,5-16,22-23 after eighteen months. Relevant future provisions: rules 6 and 8(3) contain one-year retention requirements; rule 7 distinguishes initial breach intimation from detailed information within 72 hours; rules 10-12 cover verifiable guardian consent and conditional exemptions. Fourth Schedule Part A item 3 limits the educational-institution exception to specified tracking/monitoring purposes. It is not blanket permission for employer disclosures. These requirements are future design inputs, not current compliance claims.

The [December corrigendum](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/3c7ebbae0e5456f493f486e6845df86b.pdf) (SUH-104), G.S.R.892(E), corrects wording and cross-reference lettering, including the publication wording in rule 1. The [October 2026 order](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/10/daf495a7b5798a3dfa72a944b51fc176.pdf) (SUH-105), S.O.5458(E), clarifies section 9(1)'s grammar and section 10's periodic data-audit wording. Neither inspected instrument changes the staged commencement schedule.

## Current security directions and remaining legal work

[CERT-In's 28 April 2022 Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf) (SUH-106), clauses (i)-(iv), require covered service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations to maintain synchronised clocks, report Annexure I incidents within six hours of noticing/notification, designate a contact and keep ICT logs securely for 180 days within India. Clause (v)'s five-year customer records requirement addresses listed infrastructure providers; it is not a universal five-year student-file rule. The actual pilot operator must establish coverage and incident responsibilities. An incident process must reconcile applicable CERT-In obligations with later DPDP duties instead of substituting one deadline for the other.

Current IT Act/SPDI applicability remains a **specific legal-review item**. The full 2011 Rules' old MeitY file returned 404 and an alternate official IndiaCode retrieval timed out (SUH-115); full-text verification remains open. On 2026-10-27 the [24 August 2011 clarification was recovered from official PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=74990) (SUH-116). It distinguishes contractual handling for another legal entity from a direct relationship with the information provider when discussing rules 5 and 6, and addresses organisational privacy policy and electronic consent. Its application depends on the operator's actual role and contracts; it does not establish that this proposed service is exempt. Do not treat every student field as SPDI or infer that the DPDP transition removes current protections.

## Electronic evidence and credential recognition

The recovered contributor ledger's general section 65B reference needs qualification. The [official Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam consolidation](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/20063/1/aa202347.pdf) (SUH-117) records commencement on 1 July 2024. Sections 61-63 address electronic evidence and conditional admissibility; section 170 saves specified proceedings already pending at commencement under the former Indian Evidence Act. The relevant regime therefore depends on the matter. This is a targeted text check, not an exhaustive case-law review.

A digital signature or protected audit history alone does not establish admissibility, truth or issuer mandate. Preserve source/version, original-object integrity, lawful access, extraction method, responsible custodian and processing history for any later authorised evidence export; legal reviewers determine the applicable certificate/procedure. Do not label the platform's receipt a court-admissibility certificate. The original IT Act landing page returned no substantive text and alternate official PDF retrieval timed out; complete current sections 4/5/43A/72A re-verification remains open under historical SUH-004.

## Decisions required before real records

1. Name each organisation determining purposes/means and each processor; approve contracts, sub-processors, breach roles and permitted data locations.
2. Record a lawful-purpose and authority decision for each field and recipient. An institution reporting mandate, provider OAuth permission and employer verification request are three different matters.
3. Resolve minors and guardian cases, including accessibility and assisted service; avoid requiring a new Aadhaar capture process as a shortcut.
4. Sign a retention schedule reconciling current requirements, the future Rules, institutional records duties, disputes, litigation holds, backup deletion and vendor obligations.
5. Confirm current statutory amendments, applicable circulars, government notifications and sector requirements at pilot entry. This was a targeted research pass, not an exhaustive legal search.

These open decisions do not prevent a synthetic demonstration. They prevent describing that demonstration as legally approved deployment.
